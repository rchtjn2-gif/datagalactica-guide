# How to see, correct or remove what a registry holds about you

Public-record registries collect information that is already published elsewhere, such as company filings, government lists and professional directories, and organise it so it can be searched. That is useful for verifying businesses and people, but it also means a registry may hold a record about you. You have a right to know what it says and to ask for changes.

## Why registries exist

- **Verification.** Lenders, employers and buyers need to confirm that a company or professional is real.
- **Deduplication.** A permanent ID lets different systems agree that two records are the same entity.
- **Research.** Journalists, analysts and developers use structured public data.

A good registry is clear about its sources, lets people check their own data, and fixes errors quickly.

## Step 2: decide what to do with each record

Once you can see your records, you usually have four options:

| Option | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The record is about you and broadly right; claiming lets you keep it accurate |
| **Correct** | A detail is wrong or out of date (job title, company, location) |
| **Hide** | You don't want the record shown publicly |
| **Request removal** | You want the record deleted |

You can also create your own Galactica ID, a permanent identifier you control.

## Step 3: understand grades and sources

Person records may carry a grade that reflects how well the record is supported by sources. A low grade doesn't mean the information is false; it means fewer independent sources confirm it. Check which sources a record cites before asking for a correction, and say which source is wrong if you can.

## Your rights

Under India's data protection law, people have rights to access personal data held about them, to have inaccurate data corrected, and to have data erased where it is no longer needed. If a registry does not respond, keep copies of your requests and escalate through its stated grievance contact.

## Good habits

- Check registries that matter in your industry once a year.
- Keep your public professional profiles consistent; registries often copy from them.
- When you change jobs, update or claim your records so old details don't follow you.
