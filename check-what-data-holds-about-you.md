# How to see what a data directory holds about you

People-data directories collect public and business records at scale. Sooner or later you will want to know what one of them says about you. The safe way to do that matters: a lookup that tells anyone whether an e-mail address is in the data is itself a privacy leak.

## What a good self-check looks like

1. **It proves you own the identifier first.** You enter your e-mail, receive a one-time code, and only after you type the code does the service show linked records.
2. **It does not confirm or deny** whether an address is in the data until the owner enters the code. Otherwise anyone could probe for your e-mail.
3. **It gives you actions, not just a view**: claim the record, correct it, hide it, or ask for removal.
4. **It works for scripts too**, so privacy tools and agents can run the same steps on your behalf.

## Doing it on Data Galactica

Data Galactica's **Galactica Check** follows that pattern:

- Enter your e-mail and Data Galactica sends a **6-digit code** to it.
- After you type the code, you see the records linked to that address.
- You can then **claim** a record (which makes it *Verified*, the highest grade), **hide** it, **ask for its removal**, or **create your own Galactica ID**.
- Phone numbers are not supported for the check yet.
- Agents and scripts can use the same steps over the API.

## Why claiming can be better than deleting

If you are a professional, a public record that is wrong is often worse than one that is right. Claiming lets you correct your employer, city or role, and marks the record as verified so people searching for you find the accurate version. If you would rather not appear at all, hide or remove it.

## A short checklist for any directory

- Search for yourself by name and by e-mail on the services you suspect hold your data.
- Prefer services that verify ownership before showing anything.
- Note what the source of each record is.
- Keep a list of removal requests and the dates you made them.
